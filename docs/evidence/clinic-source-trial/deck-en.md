# Keep the Follow-Up Thread

### A clinic buyer-hypothesis exercise

**FICTIONAL / UNVERIFIED — SYNTHETIC EXERCISE ONLY**

No real clinic, staff, or patient data. No buyer validation or outcome evidence.

---

## The hypothesis—not a finding

Clinic staff **may** lose follow-up continuity between inquiry, booking, and visit.

The question is whether clearer ownership and a staff-reviewable workflow would fit existing work—not whether automation is automatically desirable.

**Test ownership, workflow fit, and escalation needs before proposing a pilot.**

## First understand today's workflow

1. What happens from first inquiry to booking?
2. Who owns each next step—and where does ownership change?
3. What happens after cancellation, no-show, or a visit?
4. Which tools and handoffs are already in use?
5. What makes follow-up hard, or already work well?

Use a recent example without patient-identifying details. Treat explanations as participant-reported until corroborated.

---

## Staff review is a design requirement

- Staff must review and approve proposed outbound communications and workflow actions.
- Show the responsible role, context, and next step before approval.
- Keep an auditable human handoff; never imply that a draft was sent or an action completed without confirmation.
- Ask staff what must remain manual and what would make the workflow unacceptable.

### No autonomous patient-facing or clinical decisions

---

## Clinical escalation is explicit

- Ask the clinic to identify its existing escalation policy, responsible role, and safe route.
- Route clinical questions or concerning situations to qualified clinic staff under that policy.
- Do not generate clinical advice, triage, diagnosis, or urgency rules in this exercise.
- If the safe route is unclear, pause the workflow and resolve that before any pilot.

---

## Unaided pricing questions

**Ask before showing a price or suggesting a budget.**

> “What would you pay for this, if anything? Please give a currency and amount. Zero is a valid answer, including USD 0.”

Then ask what the amount covers, who would approve it, and what would need to be true. Record answers as unvalidated participant statements—not willingness-to-pay evidence.

---

## Evidence boundary and next step

**Available:** only the approved synthetic brief. **Unavailable:** real workflow records, interviews, source definitions, attribution, market evidence, buyer validation, and outcomes.

Next: with authorization, interview staff and decision-makers; map roles and handoffs using fictional examples; verify review and escalation controls; seek disconfirming evidence.

**Decision:** consider a staff-supervised, narrowly scoped operational pilot only after workflow fit, ownership, privacy, and escalation are agreed. Otherwise revise or stop.
