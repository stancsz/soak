# Clinic Buyer-Hypothesis Research Note

> **FICTIONAL / UNVERIFIED — exercise material only.** The figures below are synthetic and do not describe a real clinic, staff member, patient, or outcome. No buyer validation or external research has been performed. No patient data is included.

## Hypothesis / 假设

**English hypothesis.** Clinic staff may lose follow-up continuity between an initial inquiry, booking, and visit. The cause, frequency, operational impact, ownership, workflow fit, and any appropriate response are unknown. Before proposing a pilot, test who owns each handoff, where follow-up currently happens, what staff consider useful, and what must be escalated rather than handled administratively.

**中文假设。** 诊所工作人员在咨询、预约与到诊之间，可能难以保持跟进连续性。原因、发生频率、运营影响、责任归属、流程适配性以及适当的应对方式都尚未确认。提出试点之前，应先了解各环节由谁负责、目前如何跟进、工作人员认为什么有帮助，以及哪些情况不能按行政流程处理、必须升级给临床人员。

## Buyer hypothesis and decision to test

A possible buyer is the person accountable for front-desk or care-coordination operations; the actual buyer and budget owner are unknown. The user may be an administrative staff member, coordinator, or manager; those roles are only interview hypotheses, not established facts. A possible problem is unclear follow-up ownership across handoffs. A possible product direction is a lightweight administrative follow-up aid with explicit staff review and a clear route to clinical escalation. Do not recommend, automate, or make clinical decisions.

The decision is not “buy or pilot now.” First determine whether a recurring, material workflow problem exists, whether staff and the accountable owner agree on a safe workflow, and whether any proposed aid fits existing tools and responsibilities. If not, document the mismatch and do not advance a pilot.

## Concrete research plan

1. **Recruitment and consent:** Ask the clinic decision-maker to invite relevant staff voluntarily. Explain that this is exploratory product research, not clinical care. Do not solicit patient names, contact details, records, or identifiable scenarios. Do not contact prospects as part of this source-draft exercise.
2. **Map the current workflow:** In a staff interview, ask them to walk through inquiry → booking → visit using a fictional example. Record roles, tools, handoffs, timing categories, exceptions, and existing safeguards—without patient-level data. Ask what happens when ownership is unclear.
3. **Test the hypothesis neutrally:** Ask for recent *aggregate* examples only if the clinic can share them safely and voluntarily; otherwise use hypothetical prompts. Ask what breaks, how often staff believe it happens, what they currently do, what works well, and what evidence would disconfirm the hypothesis. Do not imply that the synthetic figures are clinic data.
4. **Establish safety and boundaries:** Ask staff to identify administrative tasks versus clinical judgement. Have an appropriately qualified clinic representative define escalation triggers, recipient, urgency, and fallback when unavailable. Never infer clinical thresholds or let a concept answer clinical questions.
5. **Explore workflow fit:** Show a plain-language workflow concept only after mapping current practice. Ask what would duplicate work, create risk, require approval, or fail in a busy shift. Identify recordkeeping, privacy, accessibility, and integration requirements as questions—not claims about capabilities.
6. **Unaided pricing interview:** Before showing a price or package, ask: “What, if anything, would you pay for this in your current situation? Please state an amount and currency, including zero if that is your answer.” Follow with “Who would approve that spend, from which budget, and what would need to be true?” Record the exact spontaneous answer, currency, context, and uncertainty. **Zero / 零 / USD 0 are valid answers.** Do not anchor the response with the synthetic revenue figure.
7. **Synthesize and decide:** Separate observed statements, aggregate evidence (if any), interpretation, and unknowns. Seek disconfirming evidence. Recommend further exploration only if a recurring problem, safe escalation design, owner, and workflow fit are supported. A pilot would require separate informed approval, scope, safeguards, and evaluation design; this note does not authorize one.

## Interview prompts

- “Please walk me through what happens from a first inquiry to a visit, without using real patient details.”
- “Where, if anywhere, does follow-up ownership become unclear? What makes you think so?”
- “What currently works reliably, and what would disprove our assumption that continuity is a problem?”
- “Which situations must leave the administrative workflow and go to a qualified clinical person?”
- “What would make an added tool unusable or unsafe in your existing workflow?”
- “Without seeing a price first, what would you pay, if anything? State the amount and currency; zero is a valid answer. Who controls that budget?”
